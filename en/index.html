How it works

Four steps, no surprises

1

A 30-minute call

You show me the routine, ideally by sharing your screen and just doing it. I tell you straight away whether it is worth automating. Sometimes the honest answer is no, and I will say so on that call.

2

A fixed quote

Scope, price and deadline in writing before any work starts. Not an hourly estimate that keeps growing — a number.

3

Build

Usually one to three weeks. You get a working version around the halfway mark, so you can tell me it is wrong while it is still cheap to change.

4

Handover

You get the tool and the source code. It runs on your machines, on your side, and it keeps working whether or not we ever speak again. Thirty days of fixes afterwards at no charge; after that, changes are hourly.

FedResource Checker — registry filings and court records

Targeted export from a federal disclosure registry, plus matching court decisions for a list of companies. Handles up to 5,000 company IDs in a single run: 45 seconds per company including the pauses between requests — and nobody has to sit and watch any of it.

TV Clean — Android TV debloater

A desktop app connects to a TV over Wi-Fi and strips out the preinstalled junk: disables unwanted apps, clears cache, turns off auto-updates, sideloads APKs from your computer. About

Six careers, one skill

Thirteen years ago I was dancing on a prime-time show on national television. Then I spent four years courtside in a moose costume. Then I sat at the director’s desk broadcasting a national cup final. Then I chopped vegetables and worked my way up to sous-chef. That is the actual skill on offer here: I walk into an unfamiliar process, work out how it really runs, and build the thing that removes the boring half of it. Your business is simply the next field I am walking into.
